Truth or Consequences · Part I

Further Leads

The names still to be checked before this part of the book can call itself finished.

Five crags do not close the file on Sierra County, and we would rather say so than let the five stand in for a county we have not fully walked yet. What follows is the frontier: places named in the record that we have not confirmed one way or the other, and the sources still unopened that would settle them. None of it is confirmed climbing. Treat every name below as a lead, not a listing.

Places to check

Three names have now been searched, with a negative result worth recording rather than dropping. Palomas Gap turns up only a hiking/4x4 pass through the Caballos — the Caballo Lake–Palomas Gap trail toward Timber Mountain — with no documented rock climbing. Turtleback Mountain (Caballo Cone), the landmark peak standing directly over both TorC and Elephant Butte, is the same story: a hiking ridge, no documented climbing, and as of May 2026 its trail is reported closed indefinitely. Elephant Butte itself turns up recreation, OHV routes and camping, again with no documented rock climbing. None of the three is confirmed climbing-free — a search finding nothing is not the same as rock not existing — so all three stay on this list as leads for our own eyes, not crossed off.

Kingston past Hillsboro on the Emory Pass road, Derry, and Hatch remain named as possibilities and have not been searched at all.

One genuine ambiguity needs resolving before it goes anywhere near a map: whether Mudspring (or Mud Springs) Mountains is a distinct range from Mud Mountain, or two names for the same ground. We do not know yet, and printing them as separate would be a real error if they are not.

Bouldering

No bouldering has turned up anywhere in Sierra County so far — but no bouldering search has been run either. That is a blank, not a finding. Given how much of this county is volcanic talus and limestone scree, it would be surprising if nothing existed; it is simply unlooked-for.

Sources not yet opened

A short list of real leads, roughly in the order we would pull them. One has since been opened and is kept here only to show it is done:

  • “Truth and/or Consequences,” the 2017 Climb Abroad blog trip report — now opened; its color on the Bat Cave’s history and approach is folded into that crag’s page. It named no route grades or a full route list, though, so it does not close the file on its own.
  • Mountain Project’s “Classic Climbs” pages for Mud Mountain and Caballo Lake were checked in the gap-fill pass but did not yield star ratings — those are still missing from every crag page in this part.
  • The Bat Cave Area map page on Mountain Project — the one place we know of that would give GPS coordinates, of which we currently have none, for any crag in the county.
  • Mountain Project forum threads, where access disputes and development history for obscure areas tend to live and rarely surface in a search summary.
  • Rock Climbing New Mexico by Dennis Jackson, and any New Mexico bouldering guide — neither has been checked for Sierra County coverage.
  • Nearby-city index pages on Mountain Project (Deming, Santa Clara, Willard, and likely Las Cruces, Socorro and Silver City) that can catch crags filed outside the Truth or Consequences tree proper.

People worth finding

The Mud Mountain developer — identifiable from the named, graded plaques bolted below every route there — is probably the single best interview available for this whole project. Timmy Fairfield, Marc Beverly, John Kear and Lance Hadfield, who revived the Bat Cave in the winter of 2008/09, would each have a first-hand route list. Bryan Pletta, Dave Baltz and Doug Teague put up the county’s earliest known climbing at the Bat Cave, undated in every source we hold — dating that work would anchor this whole part’s history. And whoever named “Clever Lunar-Themed Route Name” at Luna Park is worth finding on principle alone.
