The Corridor

Montana

Limestone gulches and gneiss ridgelines at the corridor's northern end.

Montana is where the corridor’s record thins out — subalpine limestone above Bozeman, a sixteen-crag gulch near Townsend, and country around White Sulphur Springs where even we found more scenery than route data. These pages are scaffolds more than chapters: honest about what’s documented, and an open invitation to whoever finishes the fieldwork.

The rock, at least, is now described. All three areas here are carbonate or carbonate-and-intrusive: the Bridgers are Mississippian Madison Group limestone carried up on a Laramide arch that exposes fourteen formations in one range; Avalanche Gulch is very probably the same Madison, karsted and pocketed, cut into the western flank of the Big Belts; and the Castles are an intrusive centre punched into a Paleozoic carbonate section — igneous spires west, limestone hills east, and an old lead–silver–copper district at the contact.

  1. The Bridgers 5.8–5.12, skewing hard at Wolverine Bowl Subalpine limestone high above Bozeman — Ross Peak, Wolverine Bowl and Rocky Canyon, reached by approach hikes rather than roadside pullouts.
  2. Avalanche Gulch 5.4–5.10b confirmed at Nude Beach; the rest of the canyon unconfirmed Sixteen limestone crags in the Big Belt Mountains near Townsend — a real sport-climbing canyon whose route-by-route record is still mostly unwritten here.
  3. White Sulphur Springs & the Castles 5.4–5.12b at Green Canyon; the rest unconfirmed The town itself has no crags — the climbing is the Castle Mountains southeast of it, granite spires and Montana's best limestone, still barely documented online.
