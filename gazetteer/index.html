The Gazetteer

A census of every climbing area from New Mexico to Montana — where it is, what the rock is, who wrote it down.

Four states, one spine of mountains. The Gazetteer is the wide-angle lens: an area-by-area census of the climbing between the Rio Grande and the Missouri headwaters, each entry with its rock, its style, its rough size, and the guidebook that covers it properly. Route lists live in the local guides; this is the map of the territory.

  1. New Mexico Scattered and geologically restless — basalt gorges, granite backcountry, and a sandstone bouldering frontier still being written.
  2. Colorado One of the densest climbing states in the country, earning it as much from geological range — granite, sandstone, limestone, welded tuff — as from sheer volume.
  3. Wyoming A strange, ranging catalog of rock — pocketed limestone at Lander, sandbagged granite offwidths at Vedauwoo, and a columnar phonolite tower rising alone from the grassland.
  4. Montana Scattered, low-key, and geologically all over the map — granite spires, limestone gulches, and one of the densest natural ice-climbing venues in the Lower 48.
