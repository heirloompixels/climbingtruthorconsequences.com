## Geology questions

- The brief proposes **rhyolite / limestone / monzonite** for the
  Caballos and Mudspring Mountains. Limestone is confirmed for the
  Caballos. **Rhyolite and monzonite are unconfirmed.**
- **Red Rock Arroyo's rock type is entirely unknown.**
- **Luna Park's volcanic rock is unspecified** — rhyolite? welded tuff?
  basalt?
- Mindat holds several relevant locality pages (linked in
  `overview.md`); none were reachable. The New Mexico Bureau of Geology
  and Mineral Resources would be the authoritative source and was never
  consulted.

## Land management questions

**None of this was verified. All of it should be, before any approach
directions are published.**

- Which **BLM field office** administers the Caballo Mountains? (Las
  Cruces District is likely, but the field office was not confirmed.)
- **Mine claim status** along the Bat Cave approach — Mountain Project
  warns to stay on public land. What is the actual boundary?
- Which **National Forest** and ranger district covers Luna Park and Red
  Rock Arroyo? (Gila is likely; unconfirmed.)
- Does any **New Mexico State Trust land** intervene on any approach? A
  recreational access permit is required if so. **This was not checked
  for a single crag** and is the most likely way to get an approach
  description wrong in this state.
- **Percha Creek**: who owns the crag and the rock wall the approach
  hops? Hillsboro is an old mining town with patented private ground.
- **Mud Mountain**: land status completely unknown, and it sits right
  against the city.
