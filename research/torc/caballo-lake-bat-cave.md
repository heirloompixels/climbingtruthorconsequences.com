# Caballo Lake & The Bat Cave

The biggest rock in Sierra County. West-facing limestone cliffs stand
above Caballo Lake in the Caballo Mountains, running from about **100
feet to close to 300 feet or taller**, with single- and multi-pitch
sport climbing from **5.6 to 5.13**. The centerpiece is **the Bat Cave**
itself — a cave starting roughly 30 feet off the ground, perhaps 100
feet tall, capped by a triangular roof as much as 30 feet deep. Mountain
Project calls it "a very spectacular looking cave with great looking
rock."

This is also the one crag in the county with a recovered development
history, and it is a good one: a handful of old mixed routes, two
decades of silence, then a burst of development in the winter of
2008/2009 involving some serious names.

The **Bat Cave Area** is described as "currently the main developed
climbing area above Caballo Lake" — which implies the wider Caballo Lake
parent area holds undeveloped or lightly developed cliffs beyond it.
That is a lead worth pulling.

## Location & Approach

In the **Caballo Mountains**, a range in Sierra and Doña Ana Counties
lying **east of the Rio Grande and Caballo Lake**. Caballo Lake is
roughly 15–20 miles south of Truth or Consequences on I-25.

**High-clearance and four-wheel-drive vehicles are required** to reach
the end of the road that approaches these climbs. The roads are rough.

## Rock & Style

- **Rock:** limestone. Quality is explicitly mixed — it "varies from
  great to pretty bad onion skin." Expect to be selective.
- **Cliff height:** ~100 ft to close to 300 ft or taller.
- **Style:** sport, **single and multi-pitch**. A handful of older
  **mixed** routes exist at a small cliff in the area (see History).
- **Grade range:** 5.6 to 5.13.
- **Aspect:** **west-facing.**

## Season

**West-facing**, so: morning shade, afternoon sun. Combined with the low
desert elevation, that points to a **cool-season crag** — fall through
spring — with mornings the play in warm weather and afternoons the play
in cold weather.

> **Inferred, not sourced.** No explicit seasonal guidance was captured

> and usable outside that window, but that is speculation.

## Access

The most access-sensitive crag found in the county.

- Most of the **Caballo Mountains** are **Bureau of Land Management**
  land. (The Las Cruces District would be the administering office —
  **the specific field office was not confirmed**.)
- **There are nearby mine claims.** Mountain Project explicitly warns
  that visitors "should take care to stay on public land when visiting
  The Bat Cave." The Caballo Mountains have a documented mining district
  and active or historic claims; on BLM land these are real legal
  interests, not decorative. Getting the boundary right matters here.
- **Dispersed camping is available** at the site.
- 4WD / high clearance required on the approach road.

**To verify before publishing:** the exact BLM field office, current
claim status along the approach, and whether any State Trust land
intervenes (a recreational access permit would be required if so). None
of this was checked.

## History

The one solid history recovered in this county, quoted closely from
Mountain Project:

> Many years ago **Bryan Pletta, Dave Baltz and Doug Teague** put up a
> handful of **mixed routes** at a small cliff in the Bat Cave area.

The area then **sat for many years without further development** until
the **winter of 2008/2009**, when **Timmy Fairfield, John Kear, Marc
Beverly and Lance Hadfield**, among others, started to add routes.

Notes for follow-up:

- "Many years ago" is undated. The original Pletta/Baltz/Teague routes
  are the county's earliest recorded climbing and dating them would
  anchor the whole history section of the guidebook.
- Timmy Fairfield and Marc Beverly are both well-known New Mexico
  climbers with substantial published records; either would likely
  talk, and both would know the 2008/09 route list first-hand.
- "Among others" conceals names. Worth asking.
