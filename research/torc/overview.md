# Climbing Around Truth or Consequences — Research Overview

Scaffold assembled 2026-08-21. **This is an incomplete first pass.** Six
web searches confirmed that a real, non-trivial climbing scene exists
around Truth or Consequences and mapped its area tree on Mountain
Project. Route-level detail (grades, lengths, bolt counts, stars, FA
credits, descriptions) was **not** retrievable — see
[Research status and limits](#research-status-and-limits). Nothing in
these files is invented; where the record is blank it says so.

Sierra County's own tourism site frames the scene accurately: the county
"is home to a few different rock climbing crags, all within an hour of
Truth or Consequences."

## Geography

Truth or Consequences (TorC) sits on the Rio Grande in Sierra County,
south-central New Mexico, on I-25 between Socorro and Las Cruces. The
climbing found so far falls into two geologic families in two different
directions:

- **Limestone, south and east of town** — Mud Mountain, immediately
  overlooking TorC, and the much larger cliffs above **Caballo Lake**
  in the Caballo Mountains.
- **Volcanic rock, west and north of town** — Percha Creek near
  Hillsboro (andesite) and Luna Park (described only as "quality
  volcanic rock"), out toward the Black Range / Gila edge, along with
  Red Rock Arroyo.

The **Caballo Mountains** are a range in Sierra and Doña Ana Counties,
east of the Rio Grande and Caballo Lake. Most of the range is Bureau of
Land Management land.

## Season

The pattern that emerges is a **winter and shoulder-season** area — the
low desert around TorC is too hot in summer, and the limestone crags are
deliberately sun-oriented.

- **Mud Mountain** is described outright as "a beautiful winter
  limestone crag." Its best wall (Winter Wall) faces **south** and gets
  sun most of the day; shade creeps in left to right starting around
  2:00pm in December.
- **Caballo Lake / Bat Cave** cliffs are **west-facing** — afternoon
  sun, morning shade.
- Percha Creek, Luna Park and Red Rock Arroyo sit higher and further
  west toward the Black Range, so they likely run later into spring and
  through fall, but **no seasonal guidance was captured for them**.

## History

The only development history recovered is for the **Bat Cave area above
Caballo Lake**, and it is quoted closely from Mountain Project:

> Many years ago Bryan Pletta, Dave Baltz and Doug Teague put up a
> handful of mixed routes at a small cliff in the Bat Cave area.

The area then sat for many years without further development until the
**winter of 2008/2009**, when **Timmy Fairfield, John Kear, Marc Beverly
and Lance Hadfield**, among others, began adding routes.

No development history was recovered for Mud Mountain, Percha Creek,
Luna Park or Red Rock Arroyo. Mud Mountain's routes reportedly carry
**plaques with names and grades below each route**, which suggests an
organized, identifiable developer or group worth tracking down — that
person is probably the single most valuable interview for this project.

## Land management

Partially established, and this needs real work before anything is
published:

- Most of the **Caballo Mountains** are **BLM** land (Las Cruces
  District would be the administering office, though the specific field
  office was not confirmed).
- The Bat Cave approach passes **active or historic mine claims**;
  Mountain Project warns visitors to "take care to stay on public land."
  Mining claims on BLM land are a genuine access complication here, not
  a formality.
- **Red Rock Arroyo** and **Luna Park** are reached by **Forest Service
  roads** (FSR #139, FSR #225) and Luna Park has a **campground**,
  indicating **Gila National Forest** or adjacent USFS administration.
- **Percha Creek** near Hillsboro is approached from a public road
  pullout, hopping a rock wall — ownership at the crag itself is
  **unverified** and could well be private or mixed.
- Dispersed camping is available at the Bat Cave area.

New Mexico **State Trust land** — which requires a recreational access
permit and is a real trap for climbers in this part of the state — was
**not checked at all** against any of these crags. Do this before
publishing approach directions.

## Research status and limits

**Read this before trusting any gap in these files as a negative
result.**

The session hit three hard walls:

1. **WebFetch is egress-blocked.** Every direct page fetch —
   mountainproject.com, thecrag.com, sierracountynewmexico.info,
   en.wikipedia.org — returned `EGRESS_BLOCKED` / HTTP 403 from the
   policy proxy. Not a rate limit or a robots issue; an organization
   egress policy.
2. **The web search budget was exhausted at 200/200 calls**, six
   searches into this task. No further searching was possible.
3. **The only reachable host is `raw.githubusercontent.com`.** The
   OpenBeta project (a CC0 mirror of Mountain Project route data) was
   tried as a backdoor; its `climbing-data` repo README now states
   "Raw data files are temporarily unavailable," the historical
   dataset filenames all 404, and repo tarballs, `api.github.com` and
   the GitHub code search tool are all blocked or scoped away.

Consequently the following were **not investigated at all** and must not
be read as "does not exist":

- theCrag.com, 8a.nu, Sendage, or any European-style database
- Mountain Project forum threads, Supertopo, r/climbing, r/NewMexico
- Guidebooks: *Rock Climbing New Mexico* (Dennis Jackson), any New
  Mexico bouldering guide
- New Mexico climbing organizations / local access groups
- Bouldering anywhere in the county — **no bouldering was found, but no
  bouldering search was run**
- The leads Palomas Gap, Turtleback Mountain, Elephant Butte, Kingston,
  Mudspring / Mud Springs Mountains, Derry, and Hatch
- Anything in the Black Range / Gila edge beyond the three volcanic
  crags listed above

See `unverified-leads.md` for the full outstanding list and a suggested
order of attack.
