# Percha Creek (Hillsboro)

A small andesite crag on Percha Creek near **Hillsboro**, west of Truth
or Consequences on the road toward Kingston and the Black Range. Mixed
crack and bolted faces, with a **five minute approach** — the most
casual approach of any crag found in the county.

Filed on Mountain Project as "Percha Creek, Hillsboro."

**Mountain Project:** https://www.mountainproject.com/area/105789954/percha-creek-hillsboro

Note the low Mountain Project area ID (`105789954`) — lower than Caballo
Lake's. This is one of the oldest pages in the Truth or Consequences
tree, so the crag has likely been known for a long time.

## Location & Approach

Near **Hillsboro, New Mexico**, roughly 30 miles west/southwest of TorC
via NM-152 (the Kingston / Emory Pass road). The approach text recovered
is specific and worth quoting closely:

> Park at a **pullout just before the bridge**, with a **sign for the
> old Percha Creek bridge**. **Hop over a rock wall**, go down to the
> creek, and **walk right a few hundred feet** to reach the climbs on
> the **east side of the creek**.

**Approach time: about five minutes.**

## Rock & Style

- **Rock:** **andesite** — a volcanic rock, unlike the limestone crags
  east of the Rio Grande.
- **Style:** **mixed crack and bolted faces.** This is the only crag
  found in the county with meaningful **trad / crack climbing**, which
  makes it disproportionately important to a guidebook that would
  otherwise be all-sport.
- **Length / height:** unknown. Nothing suggests multi-pitch.

## Access

**Unverified and potentially sensitive.**

The approach involves parking at a public pullout and **hopping a rock
wall** to get to the creek. A rock wall is a boundary of some kind. No
land-ownership information was recovered for the crag or the approach,
and Hillsboro is an old mining settlement with a great deal of patented
private ground mixed into the surrounding public land.

**Establish ownership before publishing this approach.** Check for
private parcels, and check whether the creek bottom is BLM, Gila
National Forest, State Trust, or private.
