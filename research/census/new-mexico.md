# New Mexico Climbing Area Census

New Mexico's climbing is scattered and geologically varied rather than
concentrated around one or two famous crags: volcanic basalt canyons
cut into the Pajarito Plateau near Los Alamos and Santa Fe, granite
backcountry ranges (the Sandias, the Organs, Tres Piedras, Questa
Dome), soft sandstone and rhyolite sport crags near Socorro and
Gallup, and a fast-growing sandstone bouldering frontier around Roy in
the northeast plains. Access is a live issue almost everywhere outside
the state parks: wildfire has erased entire tuff crags (Cochiti
Mesa/Capulin Canyon), a rancher's gate closure has cut off the classic
approach to the Enchanted Tower, university land acquisition has
closed formations in the Doña Ana Mountains, and the Navajo Nation's
1966 ban on climbing anywhere on tribal land makes Shiprock legally
off-limits, not just difficult. The state has no equivalent of a single
authoritative print guidebook in print for every area — coverage is
split between an old Falcon Guides state guide, a dedicated 2016
bouldering guide for the Roy/Mills Canyon explosion, and a scatter of
locally produced/online guides (Los Alamos Mountaineers, Tom Grundy's
Enchanted Tower page) that fill in the gaps.

**Research method and limitation.** Mountain Project (mountainproject.com)
and theCrag (thecrag.com) are blocked to direct page fetches from this
research environment, so this census was built from web-search result
snippets that quote and summarize those sites (search snippets
routinely surface MP-sourced route counts, grade ranges, and area
descriptions verbatim), plus secondary sources — local guide-service
sites, newspaper outdoors columns, SummitPost, Wikipedia, and
guidebook-adjacent blogs. Every figure below traces to a specific
search result cited in Sources; nothing is estimated from memory
alone. A hard per-session web-search budget was exhausted before a
handful of areas on the target list (Cornudas Mountains, Aguirre
Springs, Sitting Bull Falls, the Gila/Silver City area, several
"other Northern/Southern Areas" catch-alls on Mountain Project's own
tree) could be independently confirmed; those are marked **UNVERIFIED**
below rather than guessed at, and Mountain Project's own page for New
Mexico was itself never successfully loaded directly — its existence
and structure are inferred from search snippets referencing it, so
treat the exact area-tree hierarchy as approximate.

## Tribal lands and closures — do not climb

- **Shiprock (Tsé Bitʼaʼí)**, San Juan County — on the Navajo Nation.
  The Navajo Nation banned all climbing on tribal land, including
  Shiprock, in 1966; the ban went from unenforced to strictly enforced
  after a fatal/serious-injury accident in March 1970, and was
  announced as "absolute, final and unconditional." Climbing (and
  even hiking/camping/driving the access roads) is illegal here. This
  applies Navajo Nation–wide, not just at Shiprock.

- Mountain Project's own catch-all regions, **"New Mexico, other
  Northern Areas"** and **"New Mexico, other Southern Areas,"**
  clearly exist (both surfaced directly in search results) but their
  contents were not enumerated. Expect additional minor/obscure areas
