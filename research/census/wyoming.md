# Wyoming Climbing Areas: A Census

## Wyoming's climbing character

Wyoming is a state of extremes and a strange, ranging catalog of rock.
In the Bighorn Mountains and around Lander, bone-white Madison
limestone and Bighorn dolomite give up pocketed, steep sport climbing
that draws destination traffic every summer. In the southeast corner,
Sherman Granite — coarse, feldspar-studded Precambrian granite —
erupts into the wind-scoured domes of Vedauwoo, a wide-crack and
offwidth proving ground unlike almost anywhere else in the country.
Devils Tower rises alone out of the Black Hills grassland, a laccolithic
plug fluted into parallel cracks that has drawn crack climbers since
1937. To the west, the Wind River Range and the Tetons carry some of
the best alpine granite in the Lower 48, with approaches that run from
a stiff day hike to a multi-day wilderness expedition. Casper and Cody
add canyon and gorge climbing on granite and limestone respectively,
and Cody's South Fork of the Shoshone is one of the most storied ice
climbing testing grounds in North America. Public land is the rule
almost everywhere — national forest, BLM, state park, and one national
monument — but enclaves of private land are scattered through it,
especially around the Sweetwater Rocks, and access depends on
respecting the boundary between the two.

The season is short at altitude and long at the canyons: Fremont Canyon
and Guernsey's south-facing limestone climb through much of the winter,
while the Wind Rivers and the Snowy Range hold snow into July. Raptor
nesting closures, grazing-season road closures, and one long-standing
voluntary June closure at Devils Tower (observed out of respect for the
significance of the Tower to many Northern Plains tribes, for whom June
carries ceremonial importance) all shape when a given wall is fair game.

**A note on method and confidence.** This census was compiled by
web search rather than by directly browsing Mountain Project's or
theCrag's area-tree pages — both sites were unreachable from the
research environment used to build this document (blocked at the
network level), so their area trees, route lists, and exact route
counts could not be paged through directly. Everything below instead
comes from search-engine snippets of Mountain Project, theCrag,
guidebook-publisher, land-manager, and guide-service pages, cross-checked
against each other where more than one source existed. Route counts and
grade spreads are therefore approximate and, in several cases, drawn
from a single source rather than confirmed twice. Entries marked
**[unverified]** rest on thinner evidence — a single passing mention,
or a name that returned no substantive climbing-specific source at all
— and a few names in the assignment brief that returned no evidence of
established climbing (Baggot Rocks as a climbing destination, Glendo
State Park, Flaming Gorge/Green River) are flagged as such rather than
populated with invented numbers.

## Lander area

Lander is Wyoming's sport-climbing capital and home to the
International Climbers' Festival every July. The climbing spreads
across a band of Madison Limestone in and above Sinks Canyon State Park
southwest of town, with Wild Iris — higher, cooler, and more remote —
as the marquee crag.

| Area | Nearest town | Rock | Style | ~Routes | Grades | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wild Iris | Lander (~24 mi SW) | Dolomite/limestone | Sport, pockets | Several hundred across OK Corral, Main Wall, Aspen Glade, Zorro Wall | 5.4–5.14d | High (~9,000 ft), aspen-and-wildflower setting; the reference pocket-pulling crag in the region. Shoshone National Forest. |
| Sinks Canyon | Lander (~10 min) | Limestone (some sandstone, granite nearby) | Predominantly sport, some trad | ~800 total in the canyon (one source tallies 77 routes at 5.11, 89 at 5.12, 40 at 5.13, 3 at 5.14 on Mountain Project) | 5.7–5.14 | Named for the Popo Agie River's disappearance and reappearance ("The Sinks" / "The Rise"); short approaches, Sinks Canyon State Park plus adjoining Shoshone NF. |
| Fossil Hill | Lander area, above Sinks | Limestone | Sport | ~60–80 across West (18), Central (25), East (36) sub-sectors, plus The Hideout (~12) | Mostly 5.9–5.12, concentration of 5.10s | West-facing, good for fall/late-summer evenings; a voluntary seasonal raptor/wildlife closure has applied roughly June 9–Aug 15 in past years — confirm current dates locally. | Area | Nearest town | Rock | Style | ~Routes | Grades | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Devils Tower | Sundance / Hulett | Phonolite porphyry (columnar igneous) | Trad, crack (finger to offwidth), a handful of bolted lines | 200+ | 5.7–5.13a | The Durrance Route (5.7, Grade III, 6 pitches, FA Durrance/Butterworth 1938) carries roughly 85% of summit traffic and is a 50 Classic Climbs of North America route; widely considered stout for its grade. ## Areas checked but not confirmed as established climbing destinations

- **Baggot Rocks** (Carbon County, near Encampment/Riverside) — real granite outcrop and the geologic type locality for "Baggot Rocks Granite," and a Wyoming Game & Fish public access area, but every source found addresses it as a fishing-access/geology site, not a climbing destination. **No evidence of an established route inventory was found; treat as unverified for climbing purposes rather than assume routes exist.**
- **Glendo State Park** — the park is real and well documented for hiking, boating, and trails, but no climbing-specific source (Mountain Project listing, guidebook, or otherwise) turned up. **Dropped from the table format; flagged rather than populated with invented data.**
- **Flaming Gorge / Green River (WY side)** — the recreation area and scenic byway are real, but nothing found ties it to an established rock climbing scene; the Wyoming-side literature is all hiking, boating, and fishing. **Dropped for the same reason as above.**

If any of the three above do in fact have documented routes on Mountain Project or theCrag, they were not surfaced in this pass and should be checked directly — this census's access to those two sites was blocked in the research environment (see Method note above).
