# Climbing Near White Sulphur Springs

## Summary

White Sulphur Springs itself has **no developed climbing in town**. The
one genuinely documented developed climbing area in the vicinity is the
**Castle Mountains**, a range just southeast of town with its own
Mountain Project area page, granite spires on its west side and limestone
to the east, and a small, locally-driven development history. The other
places named in the original research brief — **Kings Hill / the Little
Belt Mountains** (north of town on US-89, home to Kings Hill Pass and the
Showdown ski area) and the **Sixteen Mile** area (south toward the
Bridgers/Toston) — **did not turn up any documented rock climbing** in
this session's research. This file is honest about that: those two areas
appear to be scenic/skiing/hiking destinations near White Sulphur Springs,
not documented climbing destinations, based on what was found. That
absence should not be read as proof nothing exists there, only that
nothing surfaced in the sources reached this session.

**Caveat on sourcing:** Mountain Project could not be fetched directly
this session (blocked by the network egress proxy), so the Castle
Mountains details below come from a WebSearch snippet of the MP area page
plus a handful of general Montana-outdoors sites. No individual route
names, grades, or star ratings for the Castle Mountains were retrievable
in this session — the moderates table below is empty by necessity, not
by omission.

## Location & Approach

- The Castle Mountains sit just **southeast of White Sulphur Springs, MT**.
- Reported access: off the **north side of the range from US-12**, just
  east of White Sulphur Springs.
- Elk Peak, the range's highest point at **8,589 ft**, gives a sense of
  scale; the range's name reportedly comes from the craggy spires jutting
  from its summits.

## Rock & Style

- Two distinct rock types depending on which side of the range: **granite
  spires on the west side**, and, per one source, "some of Montana's best
  limestone" to the east.
- Three main developed climbing areas are named: **Grasshopper**,
  **Limestone Ridge**, and **Green Canyon**.
  - **Green Canyon** has the most routes of the three — approximately
    **40 developed routes**, some already bolted.
  - Grasshopper and Limestone Ridge are named but no route counts, rock
    type detail, or grade ranges for them surfaced in this session's
    sources.

## Camping & Logistics

White Sulphur Springs is the nearest town for lodging and food. No
specific campground, dispersed-camping, or trailhead-logistics
information for the Castle Mountains climbing areas was found this
session.

## Access

Access is via US-12 on the north side of the range, east of White
Sulphur Springs. No information on land ownership specifics (National
Forest vs. state vs. private), seasonal closures, or permit requirements
for the Castle Mountains climbing areas was found this session — this
should be confirmed before publishing access guidance in a guidebook.

## History

- Development in the Castle Mountains is credited mainly to **Ron and
  Gerald Brunckhorst**, along with friends and family.
- Ron Brunckhorst also authored the area's guidebook (see below).
- No further first-ascent history, dates, or broader regional-development
  narrative surfaced in this session's sources.

## Guidebooks & Resources

- **"The Big Empty," Ron Brunckhorst** — the guidebook to the Castle
  Mountains, per this session's research. Reportedly available at **Base
  Camp** (Helena) and **Spire Climbing Center** (Bozeman). Edition,
  printing date, and page count were **not verified** this session.
- **Mountain Project** — "Rock Climbing in Castle Mountains, Southwest
  Region": https://www.mountainproject.com/area/111954599/castle-mountains
- No dedicated guidebook or Mountain Project coverage was found this
  session for Kings Hill/Little Belt Mountains or Sixteen Mile as rock
  climbing destinations specifically.
