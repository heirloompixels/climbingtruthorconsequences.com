# Ten Sleep Canyon, Wyoming

## Summary

Ten Sleep Canyon is a dolomite sport-climbing destination in the Bighorn Mountains of north-central Wyoming, just east of the small town of Ten Sleep (population under 300). Over roughly the last decade it has grown from a backwater known mostly to locals into an internationally recognized sport-climbing area, with well over 1,000 routes (guidebook publisher Wolverine Publishing now cites 1,400+) spread across dozens of named crags on both sides of the canyon. The rock is Bighorn Dolomite (Ordovician-age, similar to limestone), and the climbing is defined by pockets, edges, and flakes on faces that range from slightly overhanging to dead-vertical, with generally well-spaced, climber-friendly bolting. The canyon has also been the site of a significant, still-unresolved access and ethics controversy over manufactured/chipped holds that led the U.S. Forest Service to ban new route development canyon (and forest) -wide starting in 2019.

## Location & Approach

Ten Sleep Canyon sits along the Cloud Peak Scenic Byway (US-16) in the Bighorn Mountains, immediately east of the town of Ten Sleep, WY, in the Bighorn National Forest (Powder River Ranger District). The canyon was reportedly named because it lay "ten sleeps" — ten overnight camps — between two Sioux encampments. Crags line both sides of the canyon along the highway and side roads, with approaches ranging from roadside to short hikes; the higher-elevation Crag 6 sits above Meadowlark Lake at roughly 9,000 feet, well up-canyon from the lower crags. [Mountain Project, Ten Sleep Canyon area page](https://www.mountainproject.com/area/105819641/ten-sleep-canyon); [Geology of Wyoming, Tensleep Canyon & Sweet 16](https://www.geowyo.com/tensleep-canyon--sweet-16.html)

## Rock & Style

The canyon walls are Bighorn Dolomite, an Ordovician-era formation similar in character to limestone, running for miles along both sides of the canyon. The rock is noted for its quality and for pocketed, edge- and flake-based climbing. Wall angles run from slightly overhanging (commonly cited as roughly 15 degrees overhanging on the "steep" routes) through dead-vertical to off-vertical, with kindly spaced bolts throughout most of the canyon. [Wyoming Mountain Guides](https://wyomingmountainguides.com/rock-climbing-areas/rock-climbing-in-ten-sleep-canyon/); [UKC, Tensleep Canyon: A Wilderness Paradise](https://www.ukclimbing.com/articles/skills/tensleep_canyon_a_wilderness_paradise-9697)

## Access & Ethics

Ten Sleep has gone through one of American sport climbing's most public route-manufacturing controversies of the last decade:

- Climbers began noticing extensively manufactured (chipped/glued/drilled) holds on new routes; the practice appears to date back to roughly 2010 and escalated sharply starting in 2016, with some routes reported to have as many as 20 drilled holds.
- In response, a group of about 18 anonymous climbers chopped the bolts from roughly 30 routes across the canyon, filled in manufactured holds, and padlocked the first bolt hangers on some other routes as a protest statement.
- On July 19, 2019, the U.S. Forest Service (Bighorn National Forest) issued a notice halting all new bolt installation and new climbing-route or trail development across the entire forest — a moratorium that is still shaping the canyon's development as of the sources found.
- Access Fund publicly denounced both the original hold manufacturing and the "vigilante bolt chopping," and has continued to be involved in the canyon's management discussions.
- The Bighorn Climbers' Coalition (a 501(c)(3) local climbing organization) has worked with the Forest Service's Powder River Ranger District and Access Fund toward a Climbing Management Plan intended to define acceptable route-development and rock-alteration standards going forward; a 2020s WyoFile headline ("Feds propose ending ban on climbing route development in Tensleep Canyon — again") indicates the moratorium was still an active, unresolved issue as of that reporting.
- Two sectors, **Funky Town** and **Trump Tower**, along with their access trails, are permanently closed to climbing and travel.
- Separately, **Munitions** and **Oblivion** (both in the Valhalla area) have carried a voluntary seasonal closure through August 15 for raptor nesting — check current-year raptor closures before visiting.

## History

Climbers have been on the canyon's dolomite since the early 1980s. Stan Price, described as the "godfather of Tensleep," hand-drilled and placed ten bolts to establish "Home Alone," regarded as one of the canyon's first sport routes. For decades afterward Ten Sleep remained a backwater climbed mainly by a small circle of locals. Over roughly the past decade (as of the sources consulted), it has become an international sport-climbing destination, driven by its concentration of quality routes, generally safe/well-considered bolting, and relatively easy road access. That growth is also the backdrop for the manufactured-holds controversy described above. [Bighorn Climbers' Coalition](https://bighornclimbers.org/); [Access Fund, Ten Sleep controversy](https://www.accessfund.org/latest-news/open-gate-blog/what-we-can-learn-from-the-ten-sleep-controversy)

## Sectors (Major Crags)

Named sub-areas identified in available sources (not exhaustive — the canyon has dozens of named crags across ~1,400 routes):

- **Downtown** — a popular, roadside-adjacent sector; gets afternoon shade around 2 p.m.
- **Metropolis** — one of the canyon's few "moderate meccas," with a lower section known for a notable concentration of crack lines and a handful of good 5.9 and 5.10- pitches.
- **Mondo Beyondo** — a large sector encompassing several named walls including Frat Boys, World Domination, Downpour Wall, and Hot Lixx, running up to the Chaos Buttress; skews toward harder grades (5.10b and up commonly cited) but is a major, heavily developed zone.
- **Valhalla** — a large sector that includes Superratic Pillar, Munitions, and Oblivion (the latter two under a seasonal raptor closure); mostly vertical-to-steeper quality stone, skewing to mid-5.10s and up in the routes identified this session.
- **Superratic Pillar** — within the Valhalla area; described as "very good quality stone," mostly vertical or steeper.
- **French Cattle Ranch** — shares the Valhalla trailhead/parking but sits at the highest elevation on the main wall, requiring the canyon's longest approach hike; includes the Back Forty and Big Kahuna Pillar; noted for "especially great moderates" alongside hard 5.12s/5.13s.
- **Leigh Creek** — a shaded-in-the-morning zone near Leigh Creek Falls with favorite walls including Sidewalk Buttress, Psychoactive, and Flatulence Pillar; home to moderate classics (including Water into Wine, below) and some of the canyon's longest pitches.
- **Hound Dog Crag** — home to the moderate classic Big Bear Memorial.
- **Wall of Denial** (Dry Ten Sleep Creek area) — home to Death Flake from Hell, also used as a moderate warm-up zone for harder routes nearby.
- **Crag 6** — a high-elevation (~9,000 ft) sector above Meadowlark Lake with several excellent mid-5.11 routes and, per one source, "a trio of excellent warm-ups in the 5.10 range" (route names not identified).
- **China Wall**, **House Wall**, **Cobra**, **Full Charge** — additional named sectors; Cobra and Full Charge are noted for good rock quality, shade most of the day, a moderate approach, and cell service.
- **Funky Town**, **Trump Tower** — permanently closed to climbing/access (see Access & Ethics).

Note: the sector name **"School House"** could not be confirmed to exist in Ten Sleep Canyon in the sources available this session; a search turned up a "House Wall" instead. Verify against Mountain Project/the current guidebook before using "School House" in copy — it may be a misremembered name, a very new/obscure sector, or not a real Ten Sleep sector at all.
