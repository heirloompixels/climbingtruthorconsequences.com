# Lander, Wyoming

## Summary

Lander is a small (~8,000-person) town in west-central Wyoming that serves as the base for a cluster of distinct climbing areas — most notably Wild Iris (high-altitude limestone sport climbing) and Sinks Canyon (a multi-rock-type canyon combining sandstone, limestone, and granite). Lander markets itself as a nearly year-round climbing destination (one source cites 320 climbable days per year) and hosts the International Climbers' Festival. ## Location & Approach

Lander sits in west-central Wyoming, roughly a three-hour drive from Jackson, WY. The area's climbing is split between two zones near town:

- **Sinks Canyon** — immediately outside Lander, running up into the Wind River Range foothills.
- **Wild Iris** — a line of limestone crags below South Pass, about a 30-minute drive from Lander, followed by roughly a 1-mile hike from the parking area to the crags, at an elevation of about 9,000 feet.

[Exum Mountain Guides, "Sinks Canyon & Wild Iris"](https://exumguides.com/ublminxportfolios/sinks-canyon-wild-iris/); [Wild Iris Mountain Sports, "Recreation"](https://www.wildirisclimbing.com/recreation); [Wind River Climbing Guides, "Climbing Areas in Lander WY"](https://www.windriverclimbing.com/where-we-climb)

## Rock & Style

- **Sinks Canyon** has three distinct rock types by elevation/sub-canyon: the lower canyon is desert-like sandstone suited to crack climbing; the middle canyon is limestone with steep, bolted face climbs; the upper canyon is granite offering crack, face, and slab climbing from beginner through expert levels.
- **Wild Iris** is white limestone, described as suited to every ability level but "best suited for moderate and advanced climbers," with over 300 bolted sport routes and a reputation for steep faces and overhangs.

[Exum Mountain Guides, "Sinks Canyon & Wild Iris"](https://exumguides.com/ublminxportfolios/sinks-canyon-wild-iris/); [Wild Iris Mountain Sports, "Climbing"](https://www.wildirisclimbing.com/climbing-1)

## Season

Lander is marketed as a year-round climbing destination: one source states the area averages 320 climbable days per year, attributing this to the combination of sun-exposed rock in Sinks Canyon and the high-altitude limestone at Wild Iris (which stays cooler in summer). Specific month-by-month or crag-by-crag seasonal guidance was not obtained this session.
