# The Bridgers (Bridger Range)

## Summary

"The Bridgers" as a Mountain Project destination refers to a scattered set of
**subalpine/alpine limestone crags high on the Bridger Range**, the ridge
that runs north–south between Bozeman and Wilsall/Maudlow along Bridger
Canyon Road (MT-86). The named sub-areas are **Ross Peak**, **Wolverine
Bowl**, and **Rocky Canyon (Morgan Cemetery)** — all reached by approach
hikes off the highway corridor, not roadside crags. This is a genuinely
different place from **Hyalite Canyon** and its **Practice Rock**, which sit
in the Gallatin Range south of Bozeman and are sometimes casually lumped in
with "Bozeman climbing" — worth being precise about, since Practice Rock is
*not* in the Bridgers. Ice/mixed climbing does happen in the Bridgers (Truman
Gulch is named as an ice venue), but Bozeman's dominant, historically
important ice-climbing destination is Hyalite Canyon, a separate range
entirely — the "ice heritage" story belongs mainly to Hyalite, not the
Bridgers, and this file does not claim otherwise.

directly in this research session (both domains were blocked by the network

## Location & Approach

- The Bridger Range runs roughly north–south between Bozeman and
  Maudlow/Wilsall, with Bridger Canyon Road (MT-86) running up its west
  flank toward Bridger Bowl Ski Area.
- **Ross Peak** (9,003 ft): about a 30-minute drive from downtown Bozeman
  plus a roughly 1.5-hour approach hike; Mountain Project describes
  approach times of one to three hours for Ross Peak and nearby Ainger
  Lake.
- **Wolverine Bowl**: a subalpine bowl just south of Ross Pass.
- **Rocky Canyon (Morgan Cemetery)**: northern Bridgers, on the south face
  of Blacktail Mountain, roughly 25 miles north of Bozeman. From Bozeman,
  take 19th Ave north out of town until it becomes Springhill Road; follow
  Springhill 12–15 miles until it turns to gravel as Rocky Mountain Road.
  Named walls inside the canyon include the Sepulcher Wall, Mushroom Wall,
  and Fusion Wall (about 3/4 mile further in).
- **Truman Gulch**: a drainage on the range used for winter ice/mixed
  approaches; details of specific ice routes/grades are unverified this
  session.

## Rock & Style

- The range is built on **Madison limestone** — described at Wolverine Bowl
  as "stunning, well-weathered blue" limestone.
- Overall the Bridgers are said to hold "about 50 miles of rock," with
  quality ranging "from extremely good to extremely poor" depending on the
  crag.
- **Wolverine Bowl** is steep, technical, sharp-edged/pocketed limestone,
  described as testing "technique but your endurance as well" — bring a
  70m rope and a rack of ~16 quickdraws for the longer routes. Of the ~48
  routes counted in the whole Bridger Range, 38 are reportedly at
  Wolverine Bowl alone, and the bulk of them sit in the 5.11–5.12 range —
  i.e., this sub-area skews hard, not moderate.
- **Ross Peak** climbing is mostly bolted multipitch sport up sharp,
  sometimes slabby limestone that tops out on the summit; rock quality is
  "generally good" but "a few pitches leave something to be desired."
- **Rocky Canyon** lower walls are noted as more **friable/chossy**
  ("kitty-litter" sections), with loose holds on the flanks of some routes
  — a genuine caution for anyone expecting Wolverine Bowl-quality rock.
- Style is predominantly **sport** at altitude (Wolverine Bowl, Ross Peak
  bolted lines), with some looser/adventurous trad-style climbing in Rocky
  Canyon.

## Season

- High-elevation crags (Ross Peak) have a short window: roughly
  **mid/late June through early October**.
- Wolverine Bowl goes into shade between about 11:45 a.m. and 2:00 p.m.,
  making it workable as a later-start or afternoon/evening summer crag.
- Winter brings ice/mixed conditions to drainages like Truman Gulch, but
  specific ice grades and conditions data were not found/verified this
  session. For dedicated ice climbing near Bozeman, Hyalite Canyon (a
  separate range) is the area's primary, far better-documented
  destination.

## Camping & Logistics

Not strongly verified this session. The range sits within Custer Gallatin
National Forest, where dispersed camping is typical in Montana forest
land, but no specific approved sites, fees, or seasonal closures for
Bridger Range trailheads (Ross Pass, Truman Gulch, Springhill/Rocky
Mountain Road) were confirmed in the sources reached. Bozeman itself, a
short drive away, has full services (gear shops, lodging, food).

## Access

- Public land: Custer Gallatin National Forest.
- Approaches run from Bridger Canyon Road (MT-86), Fairy Lake Road, and
  Battle Ridge for the higher crags; Springhill Road → Rocky Mountain Road
  (gravel) for Rocky Canyon/Morgan Cemetery.
- No specific closures, seasonal restrictions, or private-land conflicts
  for Bridger Range crags surfaced in this research; if planning a trip,
  check the current Mountain Project area page and Southwest Montana
  Climbers Coalition (SMCC) news for any updates.

## History

- Ross Peak's North Face/Northeast Face saw an early first ascent in
  **August 1975 by Gary Skaar and Jack Tackle**, per American Alpine Club
  Publications (an AAC article titled "Ross Peak, Blue Phoenix" documents
  climbing on the peak, including a route called *Blue Phoenix*, rated
  around 5.12 — well beyond "moderate," noted here for history only, not
  in the moderates table below).
- The broader Bozeman-area climbing community organized around access
  issues in the late 1990s: the **Southwest Montana Climbers Coalition
  (SMCC)** was founded by locals **Bill Dockins, Tom Kalakay, Pat Callis,
  and HJ Schmidt** after the U.S. Forest Service closed the Scorched Earth
  crag and a private landowner closed access to Allenspur. SMCC is a
  501(c)(3), an Access Fund affiliate, and has run an anchor/hardware
  replacement program and helped keep Hyalite Canyon plowed for winter ice
  access. (swmontanaclimbers.org — site itself could not be fetched
  directly this session; details are from search snippets of it and of
  third-party summaries.)
- **"Montana Alpine Club"** (named in the original research brief) was
  **not found or verified** in this session's searches. The organization
  that did surface repeatedly, and appears to be the real regional body
  relevant to guided ice/alpine climbing near Bozeman, is the **Montana
  Mountaineering Association** (mtmountaineering.org) — a distinct name
  from "Montana Alpine Club." Treat "Montana Alpine Club" as unverified
  until confirmed directly.
