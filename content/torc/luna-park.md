Luna Park lies north and west of Truth or Consequences, out in Forest
Service country toward the Black Range and the edge of the Gila, and
Mountain Project sums it up as mostly moderate sport climbing on
quality volcanic rock. It shares a road network with Red Rock Arroyo
and, unusually for this county, comes with its own small campground —
which makes it the easiest weekend destination we have found here
despite being the farthest from town. Three moderates at 5.7–5.8 sit on
a longer bolted flat face; the rest of the crag runs 5.9–5.11, and the
longest lines are on the buttress left of the slot. A further sub-area,
**Upper Park**, sits above the main crag. The route names that have
surfaced — Man in the Moon, Giant Leap, Luna Barr, and one climb
bolted, apparently without irony, as "Clever Lunar-Themed Route Name"
— read like the work of one developer with a running joke, and finding
that person is on our list.

## Rock, season & access

The specific volcanic rock here — rhyolite, welded tuff, basalt — was
never identified in the sources we could reach; given the Black Range
setting, rhyolite or tuff would be a reasonable guess, but it is a
guess, not a finding. Season is no longer a guess, though: the window
runs mid-March through May, and again September through
mid-November — shoulder seasons, the mirror image of the winter
limestone at Mud Mountain and Caballo Lake, and a structural fact worth
building a trip calendar around. Access runs by way of Forest Service
roads, almost certainly the Gila National Forest, though the specific
ranger district is unconfirmed; forest roads out here are also subject
to fire closures and monsoon washouts, so check current conditions
before you drive out. Camping is a small three-site USFS campground
right at the crag — no water, so bring your own.

## Approach

The approach is now confirmed from two directions. From the south,
take FR 225 by way of FR 139 out of Monticello. From the north, FR 139
leaves the I-25 west frontage road roughly five miles south of Exit
100 (Red Rock). An earlier approach text in our notes — east from I-25
Exit 115 on Forest Service Road #1, then south and west on FSR #225 —
no longer looks reliable next to this and may have been a conflation
with a different route in; treat the two directions above as the ones
to drive.
