Luna Park lies north and west of Truth or Consequences, out in Forest
Service country toward the Black Range and the edge of the Gila, and
Mountain Project sums it up as mostly moderate sport climbing on
quality volcanic rock. It shares a road network with Red Rock Arroyo
and, unusually for this county, comes with its own campground — which
makes it the easiest weekend destination we have found here despite
being the farthest from town. The route names that have surfaced —
Man in the Moon, Giant Leap, Zero Gravity, Luna Barr, and one climb
bolted, apparently without irony, as "Clever Lunar-Themed Route Name"
— read like the work of one developer with a running joke, and finding
that person is on our list.

## Rock, season & access

The specific volcanic rock here — rhyolite, welded tuff, basalt — was
never identified in the sources we could reach; given the Black Range
setting, rhyolite or tuff would be a reasonable guess, but it is a
guess, not a finding. The same goes for season: Luna Park sits well
above the Rio Grande valley, so a spring-through-fall window — the
mirror image of the winter limestone at Mud Mountain and Caballo
Lake — is plausible and would matter structurally to this book if
confirmed, but nothing sourced says so yet. Access runs by way of
Forest Service roads, almost certainly the Gila National Forest,
though the specific ranger district is unconfirmed; forest roads out
here are also subject to fire closures and monsoon washouts, so check
current conditions before you drive out.

## Approach

One approach text survives from the research pass — from I-25 Exit
115, east on Forest Service Road #1 for about eleven miles, then south
and west on FSR #225 to the campground — but it reads oddly (east from
an interstate exit, to reach a crag that sits west of it) and may have
been conflated with the Red Rock Arroyo approach in the same source.
**We are not printing it as reliable.** Read the area page directly,
or better, drive it, before trusting this route.
