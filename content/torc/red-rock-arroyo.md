Red Rock Arroyo is no longer quite the blank page it was. It is
**several smaller crags, both uphill and downhill of Luna Park**,
northeast of Monticello off Forest Service Road #225, on the **Cibola**
National Forest — and the rock, a source finally tells us, is volcanic
and "can be super (darker red) to rubbish (lighter buff)." One sentence,
three facts: the stone is volcanic, the name is descriptive rather than
decorative, and the good rock is literally the red rock. Route counts,
grades, style and season are still unrecorded, and this page stays a
scaffold — but it is a scaffold with a rock type on it now.

## Approach

Two approaches survive in the record, from opposite ends of the same
Forest Service road network — the crags sit along FR #225. From the
south, Forest Service Road #139 runs in from Monticello. From the north, it leaves the I-25 west frontage
road roughly five miles south of Exit 100 (Red Rock). A third approach
text, describing a route from Exit 115 to the Luna Park campground,
appears to belong to Luna Park rather than here and may have been
conflated with it in the source — treat it as Luna Park's, not this
area's. No GPS coordinates, foot approach, or vehicle requirement have
been recovered for either FSR #139 approach; read the area page
directly before you drive out.

## The rock — read the colour

Sharing a road, a forest and a hillside with Luna Park almost certainly
means sharing a geology. Luna Park sits on the southern flank of the
**San Mateo Mountains**, on rhyolite of the **Nogal Canyon caldera**
system — the 28.5 Ma Vicks Peak Tuff and the Springtime Canyon rhyolites
above it. Red Rock Arroyo's crags are the same hillside's outcrops, and
we take them to be the same ash-flow sequence, ~29–28 Ma. That is an
inference from adjacency and regional mapping, not a mapped
determination; the sheet to open is Sierra Fijardo (NMBGMR GM-87).

The red-versus-buff split almost certainly tracks **welding and
devitrification inside a cooling unit**. An ash-flow tuff cools in
bands: the middle of the sheet welds densely, oxidises and
recrystallises in the vapour phase, and comes out hard, dark and often
distinctly red; the top and bottom of the same sheet cool too fast to
weld properly and stay soft, pale and vitric. Comparable red zones are
mapped in this volcanic field — at Chupadera Peak near Socorro, 60-metre
craggy cliffs mark an unusually thick dusky-red lithophysal zone in the
basal densely welded La Jencia Tuff.

So the practical rule at Red Rock Arroyo is the one the source already
gives you, with a reason attached: **climb the red, distrust the buff.**
On poorly welded buff tuff, holds snap off, bolts loosen in their holes
over the years, and the rock softens when it is wet.

## What is still not known

Climbing style — sport, trad, bouldering, or some mix — route count,
grade range, cliff height, aspect, season and development history are
all still unrecorded, and it is not established how much roped climbing
these crags actually hold. The ranger district is now known (Magdalena);
permits, closures and the foot approach from FR #225 are not.
