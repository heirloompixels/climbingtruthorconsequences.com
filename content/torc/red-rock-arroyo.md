Red Rock Arroyo is real enough to hold its own place in the Truth or
Consequences area tree on Mountain Project, sharing a road network
with Luna Park out toward the Black Range. Beyond that, this page is
mostly a placeholder for what fieldwork still needs to establish: the
rock type, the climbing style, a route count, any sense of season. The
name suggests red rock — plausibly a rhyolite or a red-weathering
volcanic, in keeping with its volcanic neighbor Luna Park — but that
is a guess drawn from the name and the neighborhood, not a sourced
fact, and we are not printing it as one.

## Approach

Two approaches survive in the record, from opposite ends of the same
Forest Service road. From the south, Forest Service Road #139 runs in
from Monticello. From the north, it leaves the I-25 west frontage
road roughly five miles south of Exit 100 (Red Rock). A third approach
text, describing a route from Exit 115 to the Luna Park campground,
appears to belong to Luna Park rather than here and may have been
conflated with it in the source — treat it as Luna Park's, not this
area's. No GPS coordinates, foot approach, or vehicle requirement have
been recovered for either FSR #139 approach; read the area page
directly before you drive out.

## What is not yet known

Rock type, climbing style — sport, trad, bouldering, or some mix —
route count, grade range, cliff height, aspect, season, and
development history are all unrecorded for Red Rock Arroyo. It is not
even established whether this area holds roped climbing at all. Land
management is a reasonable-but-unconfirmed US Forest Service, likely
Gila National Forest given the Monticello and Black Range setting; the
specific ranger district, and any permits or closures, were never
checked.

## Routes

No route names, grades, or types have surfaced for Red Rock Arroyo in
any source we could reach. The table stays empty rather than guessed.
