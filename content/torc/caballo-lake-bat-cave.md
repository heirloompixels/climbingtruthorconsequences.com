The biggest rock in Sierra County stands above Caballo Lake in the
Caballo Mountains, fifteen to twenty miles south of town off I-25 —
west-facing limestone running from roughly a hundred feet to close to
three hundred, quality that Mountain Project itself calls honest:
great in places, "pretty bad onion skin" in others. The centerpiece is
**the Bat Cave**, a genuinely spectacular formation starting about
thirty feet up the wall, roughly a hundred feet tall, capped by a
triangular roof as deep as thirty feet. Three more sectors share the
area — Gotham City, Grey Wall, and Stuff Left of the Cave — and the
Bat Cave Area is described as only the currently developed part of a
larger Caballo Lake holding, which is a lead worth pulling on its own.

## History

This is the one corner of the county with a real, recorded history.
Years ago, **Bryan Pletta, Dave Baltz and Doug Teague** put up a
handful of mixed routes at a small cliff here — the county's earliest
known climbing, undated in every source we have found. The area then
sat untouched for decades until the winter of **2008/2009**, when
**Timmy Fairfield, John Kear, Marc Beverly and Lance Hadfield**, among
others, returned and built out the sport routing that defines the
place today. Fairfield and Beverly in particular are known quantities
in New Mexico climbing; either would likely have the full 2008/09
route list first-hand.

## Approach & access

Reaching the base requires a high-clearance or 4WD vehicle over rough
road; the exact turn set and any GPS coordinates are not yet in hand,
so we are withholding a written approach until we have driven it.
**Read this before you go regardless:** active or historic mine claims
sit near the approach, and Mountain Project explicitly warns climbers
to stay on public land. Most of the Caballo Mountains are BLM ground,
almost certainly under the Las Cruces District, but the exact
boundary along this approach has not been confirmed from here.
Dispersed camping is available at the site.
