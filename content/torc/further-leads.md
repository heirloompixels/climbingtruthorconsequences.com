Five crags do not close the file on Sierra County, and we would rather
say so than let the five stand in for a county we have not fully
walked yet. What follows is the frontier: places named in the record
that we have not confirmed one way or the other, and the sources still
unopened that would settle them. None of it is confirmed climbing.
Treat every name below as a lead, not a listing.

## Places to check

**Palomas Gap**, **Turtleback Mountain** — the landmark peak standing
directly over both TorC and Elephant Butte — **Elephant Butte** itself,
**Kingston** past Hillsboro on the Emory Pass road, **Derry**, and
**Hatch** were named as possibilities and never searched at all. A
Mountain Project search that turns up nothing for Turtleback Mountain,
in particular, would itself be worth reporting — that much visible
rock going undocumented would be notable.

One genuine ambiguity needs resolving before it goes anywhere near a
map: whether **Mudspring (or Mud Springs) Mountains** is a distinct
range from **Mud Mountain**, or two names for the same ground. We do
not know yet, and printing them as separate would be a real error if
they are not.

## Bouldering

No bouldering has turned up anywhere in Sierra County so far — but no
bouldering search has been run either. That is a blank, not a finding.
Given how much of this county is volcanic talus and limestone scree,
it would be surprising if nothing existed; it is simply unlooked-for.

## Sources not yet opened

A short list of real leads, roughly in the order we would pull them:

- **"Truth and/or Consequences,"** a 2017 trip report on the Climb
  Abroad blog — title alone suggests it is written specifically about
  this area, and it is the single highest-priority unread source on
  file.
- **Mountain Project's "Classic Climbs" pages** for Mud Mountain and
  Caballo Lake, which would supply the star ratings missing from every
  crag page in this part.
- **The Bat Cave Area map page** on Mountain Project — the one place
  we know of that would give GPS coordinates, of which we currently
  have none, for any crag in the county.
- **Mountain Project forum threads**, where access disputes and
  development history for obscure areas tend to live and rarely
  surface in a search summary.
- ***Rock Climbing New Mexico*** by Dennis Jackson, and any New Mexico
  bouldering guide — neither has been checked for Sierra County
  coverage.
- **Nearby-city index pages** on Mountain Project (Deming, Santa Clara,
  Willard, and likely Las Cruces, Socorro and Silver City) that can
  catch crags filed outside the Truth or Consequences tree proper.

## People worth finding

The **Mud Mountain developer** — identifiable from the named, graded
plaques bolted below every route there — is probably the single best
interview available for this whole project. **Timmy Fairfield, Marc
Beverly, John Kear and Lance Hadfield**, who revived the Bat Cave in
the winter of 2008/09, would each have a first-hand route list.
**Bryan Pletta, Dave Baltz and Doug Teague** put up the county's
earliest known climbing at the Bat Cave, undated in every source we
hold — dating that work would anchor this whole part's history. And
whoever named "Clever Lunar-Themed Route Name" at Luna Park is worth
finding on principle alone.
