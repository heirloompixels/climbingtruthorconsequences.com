New Mexico's climbing doesn't concentrate around one or two famous
crags the way its neighbors do — it scatters across volcanic basalt
canyons cut into the Pajarito Plateau, granite backcountry ranges (the
Sandias, the Organs, Tres Piedras, Questa Dome), soft sandstone and
rhyolite sport crags near Socorro and Gallup, and a fast-growing
sandstone bouldering frontier around Roy in the northeast plains.
Access is a live issue almost everywhere outside the state parks:
wildfire has erased entire tuff crags, a rancher's gate closure has cut
off a classic approach, university land acquisition has closed
formations near Las Cruces, and the Navajo Nation's ban on climbing
anywhere on tribal land makes Shiprock legally off-limits, not just
difficult. No single guidebook covers the state — coverage splits
between an old Falcon state guide, a dedicated bouldering guide for the
Roy explosion, and a scatter of locally produced online guides.

## Tribal lands and closures — do not climb

**Shiprock (Tsé Bitʼaʼí)**, San Juan County, is on the Navajo Nation.
The Navajo Nation banned all climbing on tribal land, including
Shiprock, in 1966; enforcement went from lax to strict after a
fatal/serious-injury accident in March 1970, and the ban was announced
as "absolute, final and unconditional." Climbing — and even hiking,
camping, or driving the access roads — is illegal here, and the ban
applies Navajo Nation–wide, not just at Shiprock. Do not climb here.

## Guidebooks

- *New Mexico Bouldering* — Owen Summerscales, 2016. Credited with
  driving Roy's bouldering growth; publisher not confirmed this
  session.
- White Rock / El Rito route guides (online) — Los Alamos Mountaineers
  (lamountaineers.org); functions as the de facto guidebook for those
  areas.
- Enchanted Tower guide page — Tom Grundy (electricant.net); an
  actively cited informal online guide.
- "Jemez Rock & Pecos Area" — distributed as a Mountain Project custom
  book listing; likely a locally-compiled guide, contents unverified.
- *Rock Climbing New Mexico* (Falcon Guides state guide), commonly
  attributed to Dennis R. Jackson — **unverified this session**,
  recalled from general publishing knowledge rather than confirmed by
  search; author, publisher, and edition year need checking against a
  physical copy.

Coverage of Diablo Canyon, the Sandias, the Box, Organ Mountains, City
of Rocks, and Mentmore likely exists in older Falcon editions or local
pamphlet guides not surfaced in this research pass.
