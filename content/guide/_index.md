This guidebook is a living scaffold. The words came first; the
hand-drawn maps, the topos and the photographs are on their way, and
every reserved plate you see is a promise. Until a page says otherwise,
treat its contents as researched from the public record — Mountain
Project, the published guidebooks, and the memories of the internet —
rather than verified underneath our own hands.

## Grades

Rock climbs are graded on the Yosemite Decimal System (5.0–5.15), with
letter grades from 5.10 upward. Boulder problems use the V-scale.
Where sources disagree we print the consensus and note the argument.

## Stars

Quality stars follow guidebook convention: one star is worth doing,
two is worth planning around, three is worth the drive from another
state. Stars in Part II are ours; stars elsewhere are the consensus of
the sources cited.

## Access and honesty

Access is fragile everywhere and especially on the small crags this
book loves. Respect closures, raptor seasons, private land and tribal
land — some areas named in the Gazetteer are documented precisely so we
can say *do not climb there*. Where our record is thin or unverified we
say so on the page.

## Sources

Every page carries its sources. The deep research files behind the
whole project live in the repository's `research/` directory, one
markdown file per area, with full bibliographies.
