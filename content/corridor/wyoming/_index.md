Wyoming is the corridor's dolomite and limestone heartland, and its
best-documented ground: Lander built a town's identity around Todd
Skinner's Wild Iris, and Ten Sleep Canyon grew from a local secret
into an international destination — and into one of American sport
climbing's hardest ethics fights. Come for the pocketed stone; read
the access history before you clip a bolt.

Both areas sit on the same arrangement of rock: a Laramide uplift that
raised an Archean core and tilted the Paleozoic carbonate section off
its flanks into cliffs. Both are climbed on Madison Limestone and
Bighorn Dolomite — and at both, climbers call all of it dolomite, which
is right about the rock and wrong about the formation. The pages
explain the difference, and where those famous pockets actually come
from.
