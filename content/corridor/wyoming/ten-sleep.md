Ten Sleep Canyon cuts through the Bighorn Mountains of north-central
Wyoming, immediately east of a ranch town of under 300 people that
took its name — so the story goes — from lying ten overnight camps
between two Sioux encampments. The rock is Bighorn Dolomite, an
Ordovician formation that climbs like limestone: pockets, edges and
flakes on walls running from a mild overhang to dead vertical, bolted
throughout with a reputation for being climber-friendly rather than
runout. Stan Price, the "godfather of Tensleep," hand-drilled ten
bolts up what's regarded as the canyon's first sport route,
*Home Alone*, and for decades afterward this stayed a backwater known
mainly to locals. Over roughly the last ten years that changed fast:
the current guidebook counts more than 1,400 routes across dozens of
named crags on both canyon walls, and Ten Sleep is now an
internationally known sport destination.

That growth has a hard edge to it. Starting around 2016, climbers
began finding extensively manufactured holds — chipped, glued,
drilled, some routes with as many as twenty — on new lines in the
canyon. In 2019 a group of roughly eighteen anonymous climbers
chopped bolts from about thirty routes and padlocked others in
protest; the U.S. Forest Service responded on July 19, 2019 by
halting all new bolting and route development across the entire
Bighorn National Forest, a moratorium the Bighorn Climbers' Coalition
and Access Fund have been working to resolve ever since. **Funky
Town** and **Trump Tower**, along with their access trails, remain
permanently closed. It's worth knowing this history before you clip
your first bolt here — it shaped, and is still shaping, what you'll
find on the wall.

## Chosen moderates

Sector placement and star counts below are as reported in
search-engine-grounded Mountain Project text, not a direct read of
the live area page — treat both as provisional until checked.

| Route | Grade | Sector | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Water into Wine | 5.9 | Leigh Creek | ~85 ft; pockets on a prominent red streak, short approach, a widely cited canyon classic. |
| Death Flake from Hell | 5.10a | Wall of Denial | ~95 ft; a steep line of massive jugs in and out of a crack/flake system — "many folks' favorite 5.10 in the canyon," and a common warm-up. |
| Big Bear Memorial | 5.10c | Hound Dog Crag | Named and graded per the record; fuller description awaits verification. |
| Boy Howdy | 5.9 | Superratic Pillar, Valhalla | Named and graded; sector confirmed, description not yet. |

**Metropolis** is called out repeatedly as one of the canyon's few
"moderate meccas" — a lower section thick with crack lines and a
handful of good 5.9 and 5.10- pitches — and **French Cattle Ranch**
is described as having "especially great moderates" alongside its
hard 5.12s and 5.13s. Neither zone yielded confirmable route names
for this pass; that's the honest gap in this table, not a judgment
that the moderates aren't there.

## Season & logistics

Shade, more than season, drives the day-to-day plan here: Downtown
gets afternoon shade around 2pm, Leigh Creek is shaded in the
morning, Cobra and Full Charge stay cool most of the day. Beyond
those notes, the canyon's broader climbing season was not confirmed
in this research pass — plan on spring through fall and check current
conditions before a trip. **Leigh Creek Campground** (USFS, riverfront,
drinking water) sits in the canyon itself; **Ten Sleep Rock Ranch** is
a privately run, climber-oriented campground with showers and a beta
pavilion; dispersed sites line the Old Road elsewhere in Bighorn
National Forest. Munitions and Oblivion, both in the Valhalla area,
carry a voluntary raptor closure through August 15 — check current-year
dates before visiting.

## The book to buy

**Louie Anderson, *Ten Sleep Canyon Climbing***, 2nd edition
(Wolverine Publishing, 2023) — the standard current guidebook,
covering 1,400+ routes; Anderson has climbed and developed in the
canyon since 1998. Pair it with Mountain Project's
[Ten Sleep Canyon](https://www.mountainproject.com/area/105819641/ten-sleep-canyon)
area pages for current beta, and read the
[Bighorn Climbers' Coalition](https://bighornclimbers.org/) site and the
[Access Fund's writeup of the manufactured-holds controversy](https://www.accessfund.org/latest-news/open-gate-blog/what-we-can-learn-from-the-ten-sleep-controversy)
before you go — the ethics history here is part of climbing the
canyon responsibly, not a footnote.
