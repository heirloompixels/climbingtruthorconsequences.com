Tres Piedras — "Three Rocks" — is a scatter of granite domes on the
southeastern shoulder of the Tusas Mountains, where that range gives
out into the Rio Grande valley thirty miles west-northwest of Taos.
The stone is old even by granite standards: the Tres Piedras
Orthogneiss, a Precambrian pluton dated to roughly 1.7 billion years,
weathered into six named formations — North, Middle, South, West,
Far and Mosaic Rock — climbing to about 200 feet at Mosaic's south
face, the tallest and most popular of the group. It is a trad
climber's crag first: friction slabs, flakes and splitter cracks,
with some bolted mixed lines on the steeper faces, and a bouldering
scene developed enough that a dedicated Mountain Project sub-area
exists for it alone — regulars joke that on a good weekend there are
as many crashpads scattered below the domes as ropes hung above them.

Land here is mostly Carson National Forest, with one small private
corner in the southwest that the landowner has historically tolerated
climbing on, waiver requested — worth confirming before you rely on
it. A raptor closure runs roughly mid-February through late May.

## Season & logistics

High desert at roughly 8,000 feet: fall through spring is the
window, with cool mornings and a chance of afternoon monsoon storms
in summer. Dispersed camping is free on the surrounding Carson
National Forest land; the nearest developed campground is at
Hopewell Lake, about 20 miles further west on US 64. The Tres Piedras
Ranger Station sits essentially at the turnoff for current conditions
and fire restrictions.

## The book to buy

**Jay Foley, *Taos Rock: Climbs & Boulders of Northern New Mexico***
(Sharp End Publishing, 2005) and **Dennis Jackson, *Rock Climbing New
Mexico*** (FalconGuides, State Rock Climbing Series) are both
described as covering Tres Piedras with essentially the same routes
and photos — either will serve. Pair either with Mountain Project's
[Tres Piedras](https://www.mountainproject.com/area/105993270/tres-piedras)
area pages for current beta, and check with the
[Carson National Forest, Tres Piedras Ranger District](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r03/carson/offices/tres-piedras-ranger-district)
for fire restrictions and road conditions before you go.
