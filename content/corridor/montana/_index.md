Montana is where the corridor's record thins out — subalpine
limestone above Bozeman, a sixteen-crag gulch near Townsend, and
country around White Sulphur Springs where even we found more
scenery than route data. These pages are scaffolds more than
chapters: honest about what's documented, and an open invitation to
whoever finishes the fieldwork.

The rock, at least, is now described. All three areas here are
carbonate or carbonate-and-intrusive: the Bridgers are Mississippian
**Madison Group** limestone carried up on a Laramide arch that exposes
fourteen formations in one range; Avalanche Gulch is very probably the
same Madison, karsted and pocketed, cut into the western flank of the
Big Belts; and the Castles are an intrusive centre punched into a
Paleozoic carbonate section — igneous spires west, limestone hills
east, and an old lead–silver–copper district at the contact.
