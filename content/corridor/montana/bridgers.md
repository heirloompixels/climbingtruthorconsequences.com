The Bridger Range runs north–south between Bozeman and
Wilsall/Maudlow, its west flank followed by Bridger Canyon Road
(MT-86) toward Bridger Bowl Ski Area, and its climbing sits well off
that highway on approach hikes rather than at roadside pullouts. This
is worth saying plainly because "Bozeman climbing" often gets used
loosely to cover Hyalite Canyon and its Practice Rock too — those sit
in the Gallatin Range, south of town, and are a genuinely different
place. The Bridgers hold three named zones: **Ross Peak** (9,003 ft,
about a 90-minute approach hike after a 30-minute drive from town),
**Wolverine Bowl** (a subalpine bowl just south of Ross Pass), and
**Rocky Canyon**, also called Morgan Cemetery, on Blacktail Mountain's
south face in the range's northern reaches. The rock throughout is
Madison limestone, "extremely good to extremely poor" depending on
which of the range's roughly 50 miles of cliff you're standing under
— Wolverine Bowl's stone is the good end of that range, described as
"stunning, well-weathered blue" limestone, while Rocky Canyon's lower
walls run friable and loose in places, a real caution rather than a
formality.

## Why we come, and what we don't yet know

Honesty first: this page is thinner than we'd like. Of the roughly 48
routes counted across the whole Bridger Range, 38 sit at Wolverine
Bowl alone, and the bulk of those run 5.11–5.12 — a genuinely
technical, sharp-edged crag that tests endurance as much as
technique, and asks for a 70m rope and a rack of around sixteen
quickdraws on its longer lines. That leaves the moderate climber
mostly at Ross Peak, where the record we could confirm is thin to the
point of being a placeholder rather than a chapter.

well documented, but sit above the moderate range this book tracks.
This page is a scaffold: it exists to send you toward Ross Peak with
honest expectations, and to be filled in properly once we or a reader
has actually climbed here.

## Season & logistics

The high-elevation crags have a short window, roughly mid/late June
through early October; Wolverine Bowl goes into shade between about
11:45am and 2pm, which makes it workable as a later-start or evening
crag on a hot day. Camping specifics for Bridger Range trailheads
were not confirmed this pass, but dispersed camping is typical
throughout Custer Gallatin National Forest land; Bozeman, a short
drive from any approach, has full services. Check the Southwest
Montana Climbers Coalition for current access notes before a trip.

## The book to buy

**Bill Dockins & Tom Kalakay, *Bozeman Rock Climbs*, 3rd edition**
(High Gravity Press, 2009) — over 600 routes across 304 pages,
covering Gallatin Canyon, Hyalite Canyon, Bear Canyon, the Madison
River area, Big Sky and the Bridger Range, with chapters on regional
history and geology. Pair it with Mountain Project's
[Bridger Range](https://www.mountainproject.com/area/107113434/bridger-range)
and [Ross Peak](https://www.mountainproject.com/area/107075098/ross-peak)
pages for current beta, and check the
[Southwest Montana Climbers Coalition](https://swmontanaclimbers.org/)
for access updates before you go.
