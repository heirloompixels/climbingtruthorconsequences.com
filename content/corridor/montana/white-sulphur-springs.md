Let's be straightforward about what this page is: White Sulphur
Springs, Montana, has no developed climbing in town, and no
documented crag turned up for either of the two other places the
original research brief pointed toward — Kings Hill and the Little
Belt Mountains north on US-89 (home to the Showdown ski area), or the
Sixteen Mile area south toward the Bridgers. Both read, in the
sources available, as scenic, hiking and skiing country rather than
climbing destinations. That absence isn't proof nothing is there —
only that nothing surfaced in this research pass, and it should not
be treated as the final word.

What *is* real and documented is the **Castle Mountains**, a range
just southeast of town, reached off US-12 on the range's north side.
Elk Peak, the high point at 8,589 feet, gives the range its name —
craggy spires jutting from the summits, granite on the west side and,
per one source, "some of Montana's best limestone" to the east.
Three developed areas are named on the record: **Grasshopper**,
**Limestone Ridge**, and **Green Canyon**, the last of which is the
most developed of the three with roughly 40 routes, some already
bolted. Development here is credited mainly to Ron and Gerald
Brunckhorst and their circle, and Ron Brunckhorst wrote the area's
only known guidebook.

## Why this page is mostly blank, on purpose

We looked for route names, grades, lengths and star ratings for the
Castle Mountains and found none that would survive being printed.
Rather than invent a chosen-moderates table to match the shape of
every other page in this book, we're leaving it out and saying so
directly: this is the thinnest-documented area in the Corridor, and
what belongs here is a season of fieldwork, not a guess dressed up as
data. If you climb here before we do, we would genuinely like to
hear about it.

## Season & logistics

No sourced information exists yet on season, camping, or land
ownership for the Castle Mountains climbing areas — access runs via
US-12 on the range's north side, east of White Sulphur Springs, but
whether that land is National Forest, state, or private was not
confirmed in this research pass, and neither were any seasonal
closures. A summer-through-early-fall window is a reasonable guess by
analogy to other central-Montana ranges at similar elevation, but it
is exactly that: a guess, not a sourced fact. White Sulphur Springs
itself is the nearest town for lodging and food.

## The book to buy

**Ron Brunckhorst, *The Big Empty*** — the guidebook to the Castle
Mountains, reportedly sold at Base Camp in Helena and Spire Climbing
Center in Bozeman; its edition, printing date and page count are
unverified, so call ahead before making a special trip for it. Start
with Mountain Project's
[Castle Mountains](https://www.mountainproject.com/area/111954599/castle-mountains)
area page — thin as it is, it's the best available online record —
and treat any land-manager or access information you find locally as
more current than anything printed here.
