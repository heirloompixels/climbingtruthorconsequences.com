Avalanche Gulch sits in the Big Belt Mountains east of Helena, closer
in practice to Townsend and Canyon Ferry Lake, inside the
Helena–Lewis and Clark National Forest. Mountain Project lists it as
a dedicated area with sixteen named crags — limestone throughout,
predominantly bolted sport with a minority of trad lines, which is
the usual character of limestone terrain. The road in shares space
with heavy OHV traffic, trucks and quads and dirt bikes, a detail
every source on this canyon mentions and worth building into your
expectations of the drive. A closely related but genuinely separate
Mountain Project area, Hellgate Gulch, sits nearby and gets mentioned
in the same breath, with larger, more exposed walls of pocketed
limestone — the two should not be confused, even though a trip often
covers both.

## Why we come, and what we don't yet know

**Nude Beach** is the crag to know here: the most developed of the
sixteen, with nearly as many routes as the other thirteen combined,
spanning roughly 5.4 to 5.10b, and the farthest sector from the road
at only a few minutes' walk. Beyond that, the record thins out fast.
Sixteen crags is a real number and this is a real canyon — but the
individual route names, grades and star ratings that would let us
recommend specific lines were mostly not retrievable in this research
pass, because Mountain Project and theCrag could not be reached
directly. We are naming that gap rather than filling it with
guesses.

## Season & logistics

Summer is described as the best window, though at least one source
suggests year-round climbing is possible at this elevation. From
Townsend, the drive in runs roughly an hour via US-12 and MT-284 to
Avalanche Gulch Road; budget for a cattle guard and culvert crossing
before the pullout that serves as the trailhead. No campground, fee,
or seasonal-closure information specific to Avalanche Gulch was
confirmed this pass — dispersed camping is typical on the surrounding
national forest land, but check current status locally. Townsend is
the nearest town for services; Helena is the regional hub.

## The book to buy

**Randall Green, *Rock Climbing Montana*** (Falcon Guides) is named
as the likely guide to this area, but its authorship and current
edition were not independently re-verified in this research pass —
confirm the printing before buying. Pair whatever edition you find
with Mountain Project's
[Avalanche Gulch](https://www.mountainproject.com/area/107984946/avalanche-gulch)
and
["Classic Climbs for Avalanche Gulch"](https://www.mountainproject.com/area/classics/107984946/avalanche-gulch)
pages, which are the right next stop for filling out this page's route
table, and check the
[Helena–Lewis and Clark National Forest](https://www.fs.usda.gov/hlcnf)
for current access notes before you go.
